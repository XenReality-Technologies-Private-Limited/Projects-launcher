Footfall Detection

Footfall Camera Placement

Counting people entering and exiting the store

1. Where to Place the Camera

Put the camera a little distance from the door. Point it straight at the door, not from the side. This way, the camera can see everyone walking in clearly. If the camera is on the side, one person can hide behind another — the camera may miss counting them.

Do not obstruct the view Do not put anything in front of the camera — no mannequins, clothing racks, or stands. Nothing should block the door or walking path.
The camera must see the door clearly. If something blocks part of the door, at least 90% of the door should still be visible.

2. How Far the Camera Should Be From the Door

The best distance is where the camera can see the whole door, plus a little extra space on both sides. If too close, it misses the full door. If too far, people look too small to count correctly.

Too Close 1 – 1.5 m Cannot see the full door. Parts of the doorway are missing from the picture. ✕ Not recommended
Correct 2.5 – 3.5 m Full door, with a little extra space, fits nicely in the frame. ✓ Ideal range
Too Far 6 m+ Door looks very small. People can't be seen clearly enough to count accurately. ✕ Not recommended
Note: The correct distance depends on the camera lens. A narrow lens needs more distance. A wide lens needs less distance.

3. Camera Height and Angle

Always mount the camera at ceiling height. In both cases, point the camera at the same spot — the door entry point.

Narrow Lens 10° – 20° Place further back and tilt down slightly. The narrower field of view is compensated by the extra distance.
Wide Lens 20° – 30° Place closer and tilt down more steeply. The wider field of view covers the door at a shorter distance.

Real-World Mistakes to Avoid

Mistakes Found in Real Stores

Examples of incorrect setups observed during field deployments

Sarath City Mall

Camera mounted too low and on the side

The person standing further away is hidden behind the person standing closer. This happens because the camera is on the side. Being mounted too low makes this problem worse — the occlusion angle increases and people block each other more completely.

AS Rao Nagar

Camera placed outside, not pointing at the store door

The camera is placed outside the store and not pointing at the store's door. Because of this, people walking into the shop next door also get counted by mistake — polluting the footfall data.

Lakeshore Mall

Side-mounted camera with partial door coverage

The camera is placed on the side, so people block each other in the picture. There is an additional problem: the camera does not see the whole door. Some people walk in through a part of the door the camera cannot see, resulting in missed counts.
