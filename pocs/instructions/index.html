NVR Requirements

The recorder must be set up correctly before we can connect the Pi. Tick every item below to confirm the NVR is ready.

What is an NVR?

An NVR (Network Video Recorder) is the brain of the camera system: the one box that collects, stores and plays back the video from every camera in the store. Our AI Edge Device connects to the NVR to read the video, so the NVR has to be set up right before we install.

Dome and bullet cameras plug into the network, the NVR records them all, and XenReality's AI Edge Device reads from the NVR. One brain, many eyes.

Types of NVR

Stores can have different recorder types. Here's how to recognise each one, and what it means for us.

Standalone NVR

A dedicated recorder box for IP cameras. The most common setup and the one we prefer. Cameras and NVR talk over the network.

Works great

DVR

An older recorder for analog cameras (coax cables instead of network cables). No ONVIF, so we usually can't read from it directly.

Needs checking

Hybrid (XVR)

A recorder that accepts both analog and IP cameras. Fine for us as long as the cameras we need are IP and ONVIF is enabled.

Needs checking

PC-based VMS

Recording software running on a computer or server instead of a dedicated box. Works if it exposes ONVIF/RTSP streams on the local network.

Needs checking
Whatever the type: it must be ONVIF compatible, sit on the same Local Area Network as our Pi, and we need a username and password to access it.

What is a MAC ID?

A MAC (Media Access Control) address is a unique hardware identifier assigned to every network device at the factory. It never changes and looks like six pairs of hex digits.

Example: B8:27:EB:4A:2C:1F — this would be the MAC ID we give the client's IT team to whitelist.

ROUTER SETTINGS Static IP setup

Static IP Address

How to Set Up a Static IP Address on the Omada ER605

Download PDF

This guide shows you how to give a device (like a Pi, NVR, or camera) a fixed IP address on the Omada ER605 router. Once set up, that device will always get the same IP address instead of a different one each time.

STEP 1: Log In to the Router

  1. Connect your computer to the store's network, using Wi-Fi or an Ethernet cable.
  2. Open a web browser and type in the router's IP address. It will look like this: http://X.X.X.1 (for example, http://192.168.1.1 or http://10.80.0.1 — the exact numbers depend on the site). You can usually find this address on a sticker on the router, or in your computer's network settings.
  3. Log in with the store's router username and password.

STEP 2: Go to the LAN Settings

  1. On the left-hand menu, click Network to open it up.
  2. Click LAN.

STEP 3: Open Address Reservation

  1. At the top of the LAN page, click the Address Reservation tab.
  2. Click the + Add button on the right to create a new rule.

STEP 4: Fill In the Device Details

Fill in the form using the guide below:

MAC AddressEnter the device's MAC address (for example, the Pi, NVR, or camera).
Binding withLeave the IP Address box checked.
IP AddressEnter the fixed IP address you want to give this device (see the format guide below).
DHCP OptionLeave this unchecked.
DescriptionGive the device a simple name, like "XenReality Pi" or "Store NVR".
Export to IP-MAC BindingCheck Enable.
IP-MAC Binding InterfaceChoose LAN.
StatusCheck Enable.
How to Pick the IP Address
The IP address you choose must follow the same pattern as the router's own address — only the last number changes.

  • Router's own address: the first number in the range (for example, 10.80.0.1) — this one is already taken by the router itself.
  • Numbers you can use: everything after that, for example 10.80.0.2 up to 10.80.0.254.
Only change the very last number (the .X part). The first three numbers must stay exactly the same as the router's address.

Example: if a store's router address is 192.168.1.1, you could give a device the address 192.168.1.150.

When you're done, click OK to save.

STEP 5: Reconnect and Double-Check

  1. Reconnect the device: unplug and plug back in the network cable on the device (or just restart it). This makes it ask the router for a new address.
  2. Check the client list: click the DHCP Client List tab at the top of the page and make sure the device shows up with its new, fixed IP address.
  3. Double-check with a ping: open Terminal (or Command Prompt) on your computer and type:
    ping <Fixed-IP-Address> If it worked, you'll see replies like: 64 bytes from 10.80.0.150: icmp_seq=0 ttl=64 time=41.891 ms
Extra Notes
  • If there's an NVR: give a fixed IP to both the AI Edge Device and the NVR. Cameras plugged straight into the NVR's PoE ports don't need their own rule on the router.
  • If there's no NVR: and the cameras connect straight to the main LAN switch, repeat Steps 3–5 for each camera on site.

NEW Pi 5 guided installation

Set up your XenReality Pi

A simple, step-by-step guide to connect your Raspberry Pi 5 with just the power adapter and one LAN cable. Click Next after each step until you're done.

What is a Raspberry Pi?

A Raspberry Pi is a tiny computer about the size of a credit card. Inside your store it works as the on-site brain for XenReality: it connects to your camera system, understands what's happening in the store, and sends insights to your dashboard.

It only needs two connections to do its job:

  • Power: the USB-C adapter that comes in the box.
  • Network: one LAN cable to your router or switch.

No screens, no keyboards, no extra wiring. If you can plug in a phone charger, you can set this up.

Choose your business type

The Pi setup is exactly the same for both. The difference is what XenReality measures for you.
First visit? Make sure the store meets the NVR & Tech Requirements first.

F&B Step 1 of 9

Check your kit

Lay everything out on a clean table. You should have:

  • Raspberry Pi 5 board
  • Official Pi 5 case (red base, white frame with fan, white lid, heatsink & feet in a small envelope)
  • 27W USB-C power adapter (comes with the Pi)
  • LAN cable
  • SD card provided by XenReality
Having a problem?

Something missing? Don't start the setup with a partial kit. Contact your XenReality point of contact and we'll ship the missing part.

Case, part 1: seat the Pi in the red base

  1. Open the accessories envelope in the case box.
  2. Peel the backing off the small heatsink and stick it onto the raised CPU (the silver square in the middle of the board). Press down firmly.
  3. Stick the four silicone feet into the marked spots under the red base.
  4. Lower the Pi into the red base. The board slides under the plastic tab at the SD-card end. Check it sits flat and every port lines up with its hole.
Having a problem?

Board won't sit flat? It's probably not tucked under the tab at the SD-card end. Lift it out, slide the SD-card end in first at an angle, then lower the port side down.

Ports don't line up? The board is in backwards. The USB/Ethernet ports must face the large openings.

Case, part 2: fan cable, frame & lid

  1. Lay the white frame (the part with the fan) upside-down next to the base.
  2. Plug the fan cable into the small 4-pin connector labeled FAN on the Pi. The connector only fits one way, so don't force it.
  3. Clip the white frame onto the red base until it clicks all around.
  4. Press the lid on top. Done! The Pi is now safe inside its case.
Having a problem?

Fan cable won't go in? Flip the connector around: it's keyed to fit only one way. Make sure it's pushed fully in.

Frame won't clip shut? The fan cable may be pinched, or the board isn't flat in the base. Re-check step 2 before pressing harder.

Insert the XenReality SD card

The SD card is the Pi's memory. All the XenReality software is already on it.

  1. Find the thin SD slot on the underside of the case, at the end with the small opening.
  2. Slide the card in with the gold contacts facing up towards the board, until it stops.
Having a problem?

Card won't go in? Don't force it. Flip it over: the card only seats fully in one orientation.

No SD card in the kit? Stop here and contact XenReality. The Pi cannot run without our card.

Connect the power adapter

  1. Plug the adapter into a wall socket, but keep the wall switch OFF for now.
  2. Plug the USB-C end into the Pi's power port: the small oval port on the side of the case (circled in the picture).

Use only the 27W adapter that came with the Pi. Phone chargers may look identical but can't power it reliably.

Having a problem?

Can't find the power port? It's the small oval (USB-C) port, the same shape as a modern phone charging port, on the side edge of the case next to the two HDMI ports.

Connect the LAN cable

  1. Click one end of the LAN cable into the Ethernet port on the Pi's case (the wide square port).
  2. Click the other end into a free port on the store's router or network switch.

This one cable is the Pi's entire connection to the world, so it must go to the same network as the store's cameras.

Having a problem?

No free port on the router? Ask the store's IT contact for a free port on a switch. Do not unplug existing cables yourself.

Cable too short? Any standard Ethernet cable works, but the Pi should sit somewhere safe, dry and ventilated, so prefer moving the Pi closer over stretching cables across walkways.

Power on and check the lights

  1. Turn the wall switch on.
  2. Press the power button on the case.
  3. Look at the Ethernet port: within a minute its little LEDs should light up and blink. Blinking = the Pi is talking to the network.
Having a problem?

No lights at all on the Pi? It's a power problem: check the wall switch, that the USB-C plug is fully in, and that the socket itself works (test with a phone charger).

Pi is on but LAN LEDs never blink? It's a network problem: re-seat both ends of the LAN cable until they click, try a different router/switch port, and check the router itself is powered.

Connect to the NVR

The NVR is the box that records all the cameras. Our Pi reads from it. All you do now is open it and log in:

  1. Ask the store's IT contact for the Pi's address (or find it yourself with the free Fing app: look for "Raspberry Pi" in the list).
  2. On a laptop or phone on the store's network, type that address into the browser.
  3. Log in with the username and password from XenReality.
Having a problem?

What is this "address"? Every device on a network gets its own number, called an IP address. It looks like 192.168.1.45. Think of it as the Pi's apartment number inside the building.

Page won't open? Make sure your laptop/phone is on the same network as the Pi (not a guest WiFi), and re-check the address. If the Pi's LAN LEDs aren't blinking, go back to step 7 first.

Only have a "guest" login? A guest account can watch cameras but can't enable remote view. Ask the client (or their IT contact) for an admin account before continuing.

Turn on Remote View

Remote view lets XenReality securely see the system from outside the store, so our team can monitor and support it without a site visit.

  1. In the NVR you just logged into, switch on Remote View in the settings.
  2. Check the camera view loads. That's it.
Having a problem?

Works in-store but Remote View won't load? The store's firewall is probably blocking the Pi. A firewall is like a security guard at the network's door, checking all traffic. Sometimes it's strict enough to block us, and Fortinet (FortiGate) is the one we see most.

The fix: whitelist the Pi (needs the client's IT admin).

  1. Log into the FortiGate admin console.
  2. Policy & Objects → Addresses → add an address object with the Pi's IP (and whitelist the Pi's MAC ID).
  3. Policy & Objects → Firewall Policy → allow traffic to/from that address (XenReality can confirm the exact ports).
  4. Save, apply, and try Remote View again.

Footfall Detection

Footfall Camera Placement

Counting people entering and exiting the store

1. Where to Place the Camera

Put the camera a little distance from the door. Point it straight at the door, not from the side. This way, the camera can see everyone walking in clearly. If the camera is on the side, one person can hide behind another — the camera may miss counting them.

Do not obstruct the viewDo not put anything in front of the camera — no mannequins, clothing racks, or stands. Nothing should block the door or walking path.
The camera must see the door clearly. If something blocks part of the door, at least 90% of the door should still be visible.

2. How Far the Camera Should Be From the Door

The best distance is where the camera can see the whole door, plus a little extra space on both sides. If too close, it misses the full door. If too far, people look too small to count correctly.

Too Close1 – 1.5 mCannot see the full door. Parts of the doorway are missing from the picture.✕ Not recommended
Correct2.5 – 3.5 mFull door, with a little extra space, fits nicely in the frame.✓ Ideal range
Too Far6 m+Door looks very small. People can't be seen clearly enough to count accurately.✕ Not recommended
Note: The correct distance depends on the camera lens. A narrow lens needs more distance. A wide lens needs less distance.

3. Camera Height and Angle

Always mount the camera at ceiling height. In both cases, point the camera at the same spot — the door entry point.

Narrow Lens10° – 20°Place further back and tilt down slightly. The narrower field of view is compensated by the extra distance.
Wide Lens20° – 30°Place closer and tilt down more steeply. The wider field of view covers the door at a shorter distance.

Real-World Mistakes to Avoid

Mistakes Found in Real Stores

Examples of incorrect setups observed during field deployments

Sarath City Mall

Camera mounted too low and on the side

The person standing further away is hidden behind the person standing closer. This happens because the camera is on the side. Being mounted too low makes this problem worse — the occlusion angle increases and people block each other more completely.

AS Rao Nagar

Camera placed outside, not pointing at the store door

The camera is placed outside the store and not pointing at the store's door. Because of this, people walking into the shop next door also get counted by mistake — polluting the footfall data.

Lakeshore Mall

Side-mounted camera with partial door coverage

The camera is placed on the side, so people block each other in the picture. There is an additional problem: the camera does not see the whole door. Some people walk in through a part of the door the camera cannot see, resulting in missed counts.
